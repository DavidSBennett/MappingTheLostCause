# The Atlanta Journal — Tuesday, December 12, 1939

Transcription of the historical-marker coverage published for the *Gone With the Wind*
premiere festival. Two scanned pages: the map page ("Points of Historical and 'GWTW'
Interest In and About Atlanta") and its continuation page. Entry 56 breaks across the
two pages. Grid references like (E-4) point to the Chamber of Commerce map printed
with the article. Unrelated articles and advertisements on the pages are not transcribed.

## Historic Points Mapped Out To Guide Festival Visitors

*Chamber of Commerce Lists Places About Which Battle of Atlanta Raged*

This map was prepared by the Chamber of Commerce to guide visitors to the "Gone
With the Wind" festival to points of historic interest in and about the city.

Only those places which have a bearing on the battles fought here, and those
associated with the "G. W. T. W." era, are listed in the following legend. Points
indicated on the map but not explained in the legend are well-known modern
buildings and landmarks.

**2.** A point in the outer line of defense works, occupied by Hardee's Corps,
July 18–22, 1864. From this position Bates' Division (Confederate) advanced to the
Battle of Peachtree Creek, July 20, 1864. North end of Piedmont Park. Remnant of
intrenched line still visible. (E-4)

### Andrews' Execution

**6.** James J. Andrews, leader of the celebrated Andrews Raid, was executed as a
spy June 7, 1862. Southeast corner Juniper and Third Streets. (P-12)

**10.** Site of Woodrow Wilson's law office, 1882–1883. Bronze tablet on side of
Ivan Allen-Marshall Building. Erected 1876. Southeast corner Marietta and Forsyth
Streets. (O-21)

**11.** Site of Hardy Ivy's Cabin. First residence built in downtown Atlanta.
Telephone Building, northeast corner Ivy Street and Auburn Avenue. (O-21)

**12.** Historic lamp post. At the northeast corner of Whitehall and Alabama
Streets. During the battle a shell shattered the base of the post. (M-21.)

**13.** Site of Marcus A. Bell residence. Known as the "Calico House." Used as
supply depot by Confederates. (Southeast corner Auburn Avenue and Courtland
Street. (P-20.)

### Governor's Mansion

**18.** Site of John H. James residence. Governor's Mansion, 1869–1921. Front wall
Henry Grady Hotel. (N-20.)

**20.** Site of Austin Leyden House. Headquarters of Major General George H.
Thomas, commander of the Federal Army of the Cumberland, 1864. Front wall
Davison-Paxon Store. (N-20)

**26.** Site of first State Capitol in Atlanta—1868-1889. Western Union Building,
southwest corner Marietta and Forsyth Streets. (M-21)

**28.** Site of Confederate arsenal and military storehouse, 1863-1864. George
Muse Company store [column continues:] Confederate barracks and prison, 1862-1864.
Peachtree Street, entrance Peachtree Arcade. (M-21)

### Railway Terminus

**36.** Site of terminus of Western & Atlantic R. R. and old Union passenger
station. (N-21).

**37.** Battlefield of Peachtree Creek. Federal position. East side Northside
Drive between Northfleet and McKinley Roads. (D-3).

**38.** Battlefield of Peachtree Creek. Point of assault on 33rd New Jersey
Regiment by Confederates. South side Collier Road at power line steel tower. (D-3).

**42.** On this spot was begun the Battle of Decatur July 22, 1864. Just inside
gates on Agnes Scott campus (I-5).

**44.** During the Battle of Decatur July 22, 1864, there was hot fighting in and
around the old Courthouse, which stood on this square. Wheeler's Cavalry made a
gallant charge here and captured a battery of federal artillery with officers and
men. Courthouse (I-5).

### Battle of Atlanta

**46.** The Cyclorama at Grant Park. This huge painting depicts the Battle of
Atlanta on July 22, 1864. (E-7.)

**52.** Fort McPherson. U. S. Military Reservation. Established on this site in
1885. Headquarters Fourth Corps Area, U. S. A. Lee Street, S. W. Four miles
southwest of city. (C-8.)

**54.** 1042 West Marietta Street, N. W. Old Dexter Niles home. Location of Gen.
Joseph E. Johnston's headquarters, July 17 and 18, 1864, where command of
Confederate Army was turned over to Gen. John B. Hood (G-4).

**56.** Intersection of McPherson and Monument Avenue, S. E. Site of McPherson
Monument, erected upon the spot where Gen. James B. McPherson, commanding the
Federal Army of the Tennessee, [continued on second page:] was killed on July 22,
1864 (G-7).

## Historic Points Mapped For Festival Visitors (continuation page)

**58.** Site of old Whitehall Tavern (C-7).

### First City Hall

**59.** Site of Atlanta's first City Hall—1853-1883. Present State Capitol site
(O-23).

**66.** Hiram Embry plantation. [column continues:] Southwest corner Collier Road
and Howell Mill Road. (D-3.)

**67.** Site of Old Mount Zion Church. 1877 Howell Mill Road, N. W. (D-3.)

**68.** Confederate Gen. Alexander P. Stewart's headquarters during Battle of
Peachtree Creek. Howell Mill Road and Bishop Street, N. W. (C-3.)

**70.** The Andrew J. Collier House, a part of which was standing during the
battle. Southwest corner Peachtree and Collier Road. (C-3.)

**71.** Near this point the formal surrender of the city by Mayor James M. Calhoun
to the Federals took place September 2, 1864. Corner Marietta and Curran Streets,
N. W. (C-4.)

**72.** Loring's Hill. Northside Drive, east side, near Collier Road.

### The Huff Place

**73.** The House of Three Flags. Confederate, Federal and British. The Huff
House. Erected 1855. 70 Huff Road, N. W., near Marietta Road. (C-4.)

**74.** Site of the Ponder House, a conspicuous brick residence on the line of
city fortifications during the siege of Atlanta. 709 Ponders Avenue, N. W. (D-4.)

**75.** Fort Hood — Northwestern salient of the city fortifications during the
siege of Atlanta. Across street from 790 Marietta Street, N. W. (D-5.)

**76.** Site of old State Street School. This eminence was crowned by a fort in
the line of city fortifications during the siege. Wallace Street, N. W., between
Marietta Street and State Street. (D-5.)

**77.** Hilltop. Site of one of the forts in line of city defense works during the
siege of Atlanta. Look west from Bankhead Avenue bridge. (D-5.)

**78.** Site of Willis Carlisle's store, 1845. The Trout House, one of Atlanta's
best known ante-bellum hotels erected on this site in 1855. Northeast corner Pryor
and Decatur Streets. (N-21.)

### Jail Was Destroyed

**84.** Site of the first Fulton County Jail, erected 1855, destroyed by the
Federal Army November, 1864. Andrews railroad raiders imprisoned here 1862.
Northeast corner Fraser and Fair Streets. (E-6.)

**88.** Old McDonough Road, now Capitol Avenue. Route taken by Hardee's Corps and
Wheeler's Cavalry (Confederate) en route to Battle of Atlanta. (E-7.)

**89.** Federal siege lines crossed Marietta Street at or near this point during
operations against the city by Sherman's army, July 22-August 25, 1864.
Intersection Marietta Street and Brady Avenue. (B-4.)

**90.** Near this spot July 22, 1864, the first Federal shell fired into the city
killed a small child. Southwest corner Ivy and Ellis Streets, N. E. (O-19.)

**91.** At or near this site stood a tall pine tree used as a signal station by
the Federal Fifteenth Corps during the Battle of Atlanta. Northwest corner DeKalb
and Moreland Avenues, N. E. (F-6.)

**96.** Site of Augustus F. Hurt House. Gen. W. T. Sherman's headquarters during
Battle of Atlanta, July 22, 1864. 1011 Cleburne Avenue, N. E. (F-6.)

**97.** De Gress' Battery—Position of Battery H, First Illinois Light Artillery,
commanded by Captain Francis De Gress, July 22, 1864. De Gress Avenue, N. E., just
north of DeKalb Avenue. (F-6.)

**99.** Battlefield of Atlanta, July 22, 1864. Site of George M. Troup Hurt House.
This was the house that appears conspicuously in the Cyclorama of the Battle of
Atlanta in Grant Park. 195 De Gress Avenue, N. E. (F-6.)

### General Gresham Hurt

**100.** Brig. General Walter Q. Gresham, Federal, severely wounded while pressing
an attack on Wheeler's Cavalry. Whiteford Avenue and Boulevard Drive, S. E. (F-6.)

**101.** Battlefield of Atlanta, July 22, 1864. Battle of Atlanta began here at
noon, July 22. Northeast corner Clay Street and Memorial Drive. (G-6.)

**103.** Point in fortified line that encircled Atlanta during the siege of the
city, July 22-August 25, 1864. Breastwork still visible. Triangle bounded by Kalb,
Bryan and Waldo Streets, S. E. (E-6.)

**104.** Monument marking the scene of the death of Major General James B.
McPherson, commander Federal Army of the Tennessee, July 22, 1864. McPherson and
Monument Avenue, S. E. (F-6.)

**105.** Fort Walker—Southeastern salient of the intrenched line of fortifications
surrounding Atlanta. Southeastern corner of Grant Park. (E-7.)

### Where Hood Watched

**107.** Site of James E. Williams house in 1864. Gen. John B. Hood (Confederate)
watched the Battle of Atlanta from the second floor of this house. Oakland
Cemetery—due north of cemetery office (E-6).

**109.** Site of the Nedom L. Angier house. Headquarters of Maj. General John M.
Schofield during occupation of Atlanta by Federal forces. Southeast corner Capitol
Square and Washington Street (E-6).

**111.** Site of Engine House No. 2, Mechanics' Fire Company. Used as a prison
during occupation of the city by Federal forces, September-November, 1864. East
side Washington Street between railroad and Hunter Streets (E-6).

**106.** Fragment of breastworks occupied by Confederate forces during the siege
of Atlanta, July 22-August 25, 1864. North of driveway leading from Augusta Avenue
entrance of Grant Park (E-7.)

**112.** Site of James M. Calhoun house. Gen. John W. Geary's headquarters during
occupation of city by the Federal forces. Northeast corner Washington and Hunter
Streets (E-6).

**114.** Site of Terry's Millpond. Severe fighting here during Battle of Atlanta.
Near Walker Monument (G-7.)
